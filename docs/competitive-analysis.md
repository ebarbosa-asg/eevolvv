## The Landscape: Three Competitor Buckets

The market trying to serve our customers is split into three categories.
None of them own the full stack. That's the gap eevolvv occupies.

---

### Bucket 1 — AI Agencies & Studios

**Who they are:** Solo consultants, small shops, and "AI automation agencies" that build GPT workflows, Zapier chains, and Make.com automations on a project basis. Hundreds of them appeared between 2023–2025. They advertise on Upwork, LinkedIn, and YouTube.

**Their pitch:** "We'll build your AI automations. 30-day project. Fixed fee."

**Their price:** $5K–$50K per project. Hourly: $80–$500/hr.

**Their model:**
- Client describes a problem
- Agency builds a solution
- Agency leaves
- Client is on their own

**The fatal flaw:** They build without diagnosing. The client tells them what to automate — which means the client can only automate what they've already thought of. The ghost work they don't know about never gets touched. And when the agency leaves, there's no one maintaining, iterating, or compounding.

**What clients say 90 days later:** "The automation kind of works, but something changed and now it's broken. We don't know how to fix it."

**How to beat them in one line:**
> "They build what you ask for. We find what you don't know to ask."

---

### Bucket 2 — AI SaaS Platforms

**Who they are:** Zapier, Make.com (Integromat), Monday AI, HubSpot AI, ClickUp AI, Lindy AI, n8n, and dozens of imitators.

**Their pitch:** "Connect your tools, build automations yourself, no code required."

**Their price:** $0–$100/month for SMBs. Enterprise plans scale from $500–$5K/month.

**Their model:**
- Self-serve platform
- You configure your own automations
- You maintain them when they break
- You figure out what to automate
- You train your team on the tool

**The fatal flaw:** They sell the shovel, not the hole. The platform is only as useful as your ability to know what to automate and how to configure it. SMBs running $5M–$50M businesses don't have a dedicated AI operations person. The CFO isn't spending Tuesday afternoons in Zapier.

In 2026 Zapier raised prices 25% for AI-heavy usage. Make processes more cheaply but requires technical sophistication most SMBs don't have.

The deeper problem: these platforms are horizontal. They connect things. They don't diagnose your operation, map your ghost work, or understand your QA compliance requirements. They can't.

**What clients say 90 days later:** "We set up a few zaps but they keep breaking. Nobody really uses it anymore."

**How to beat them in one line:**
> "They sell you the tool. We are the team that runs it."

---

### Bucket 3 — Vertical AI Specialists

**Who they are:** VC-backed startups and boutique firms that go deep in one function — AI for finance audit (Kobalt Labs, Quanta, Auditoria.AI), AI for QA and compliance, AI for legal, AI for HR. Kobalt Labs raised $11M in Dec 2025. Quanta raised $15M.

**Their pitch:** "We built an AI purpose-built for [your specific function]."

**Their price:** Custom enterprise contracts, typically $50K–$500K/year. Some SaaS models at $500–$5K/month.

**Their model:**
- Single-function focus (just audit, just QA, just accounting)
- Deep product for that one vertical
- SaaS or consulting model within that vertical
- No cross-functional visibility

**The fatal flaw:** Ghost work doesn't stay inside one function. The accountant doing manual reconciliations is also the person emailing approvals to the VP and manually logging everything into the ERP. You can't fix Tuesday afternoon by only looking at the accounting software. Vertical specialists see a slice. We see the whole business.

Additionally: most vertical AI startups are building platforms. Their incentive is to get you dependent on their SaaS, not to understand your operation at a human level and build what fits you.

**How to beat them in one line:**
> "They're deep in one function. We see the whole business."
