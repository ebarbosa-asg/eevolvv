## 2. The Villain — Ghost Work

Every great brand has an enemy. Ours is **ghost work**.

Ghost work = the invisible, habitual, pointless tasks haunting every business.
It's work that exists because it's always existed. Nobody questions it. It just is.
- The controller who reconciles two spreadsheets every Tuesday afternoon
- The VP of Sales manually logging calls into CRM after every meeting
- The ops lead copy-pasting order data between systems since 2019
- The accountant re-keying numbers from invoices into QuickBooks by hand

Ghost work is everywhere. It compounds. Businesses hire people to do ghost work.
Those people have meetings about it. They train new hires on it.

**We exorcise ghost work.**

The diagnostic is the séance — we find all the ghosts.
The 60-day engagement is the exorcism.
After eevolvv: a haunt-free operation.

Use "ghost work" in content. It's more visceral than "inefficiency." It's specific.
Business owners will immediately picture their own ghost work when they read it.

## 3. Brand Voice

Four rules. Each one has a WRONG version and a RIGHT version so you can feel the difference.

### Rule 1 — Sound like an operator, not a consultant
Wrong: "We leverage AI to drive operational efficiency across your core business functions."
Right: "You hired great people. Right now, some of them are copying and pasting. We fix that."

Wrong: "Our proprietary diagnostic process maps 12 operational nodes."
Right: "Day 1 we ask uncomfortable questions. By Day 7, you'll never look at your business the same way."

### Rule 2 — Name the specific, not the category
Wrong: "Significant time savings and cost reduction."
Right: "23 hours back. Every week. Starting week two."

Wrong: "Identified material financial risk."
Right: "Found $140K in audit exposure before it became a Q3 problem."

Wrong: "Streamlined the onboarding process."
Right: "Cut new hire onboarding from 3 weeks to 4 days. Same team. Same budget."

### Rule 3 — Short sentences hit harder than long ones
Wrong: "By implementing our comprehensive AI-driven workflow automation system, your team will be able to redirect their time and attention toward higher-value strategic initiatives."
Right: "Your team stops doing robot work. They start doing human work."

### Rule 4 — Urgency without panic
Wrong: "AI is disrupting every industry and businesses that fail to adapt will be left behind in an increasingly competitive landscape."
Right: "Your competitors are already on the other side of this. Some of them got there last year."

## 5A. Free First, No Refund Promises

**Operating rule:** eevolvv does not use money-back guarantees or refund promises as a marketing crutch. It creates the wrong buyer expectation and pulls attention away from the actual value.

**The replacement strategy:** give people genuinely useful free artifacts that make them want to come back for the deeper build.
- Free report
- Free roadmap preview
- Free workflow scan
- Free ghost-work teardown
- Free pricing sanity check

**How to frame it:**
> "We don't promise your money back. We give you something useful before you spend it."

**Volvv-E visual system:** keep free-offer art extremely subtle. A tiny spray-painted `v` tag can mark "free diagnostic" or a future "free roadmap" subpage, but avoid murals, loud slashes, big price-crossouts, or making the main page feel gimmicky.

**Homepage direction:** less noise, more action. Keep the first move obvious and let Volvv-E Easter eggs be tiny, optional details around conversion points.

**Current homepage strategy:** the main page should be short and free-report-centric. The top CTA sends people to `/diagnostic`, a high-tech but subtle report intake. The $97 roadmap is the post-report upgrade, positioned as a discounted next step after the free diagnostic proves useful. Remove filler dashboards, fake live-ops panels, and random animated metrics from the main page.

## 8. Copy Rules — Hard Stops

### Always
- "ghost work" > "inefficiency" > "manual processes"
- Story first, stat second (VP logging calls > "23 hours/week")
- Short sentences under 12 words hit harder than long ones
- "eevolvv" as a verb at least once per piece
- Specific number > general claim, always
- The CTA is the diagnostic. Always. Not "contact us."
- Write for the person, not the company. CFOs are people under pressure.

### Never
- Never: "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," "seamless," "synergies," "leverage," "unlock"
- Never: "AI automation platform" — we are a service/team, not a platform
- Never: call the diagnostic a "form" — it's an Evolution Assessment
- Never: soften the urgency. The Darwin is real.
- Never: describe the AI stack to clients. They care about outcomes, not models.
- Never: publish without one hard number (hours, dollars, days, percentage)
- Never: write copy a competitor could swap their name into. If you could, rewrite it.

### The specificity test
Before publishing: can you make it more specific?
"Saved time" → "saved 23 hours" → "saved 23 hours/week starting week two" → "saved 23 hours/week in a 12-person ops team starting week two"
Go as specific as the facts allow.
